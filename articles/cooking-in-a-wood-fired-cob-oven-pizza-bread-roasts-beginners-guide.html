Cooking in a Wood-Fired Oven: A Beginner's Guide to Pizza, Bread and Roasts on One Firing

A wood-fired oven stores heat in its walls, so one fire can cook for hours. The trick is cooking in the right order as the temperature falls: pizza first, then roasts, then bread and slow dishes.

The cooking order

StageOven temperatureGood for
Fire in the oven650 F and up (floor about 650 to 700 F on an infrared thermometer)Pizza, flatbreads
RoastingAbout 450 to 600 FMeat and vegetables in a hot pan, with a medium bed of coals and a small 2 to 4 inch flame, door off for under an hour or partly closed for longer roasts
Baking500 F and belowBread, desserts, beans, pasta and smaller roasts, after raking out the coals, brushing the floor and shutting the door tightly

Wood-fired oven makers describe the order as pizza first, then roasting, then baking, because the oven loses heat steadily once the fire is out.

How big a fire?

You can only get out the heat you put in. For a weeknight pizza, fire only until the dome turns white (the soot burns off). For a big baking day, fire longer. An oven that is pizza-ready in about 45 minutes usually takes 7 or 8 sticks of wood, adding one piece every 15 to 20 minutes.

Practical tips

  • Rotate pans. The side nearest the fire or coals runs hotter.
  • Use heavy pans. Cast iron skillets and Dutch ovens handle the heat; preheat them for sear marks.
  • Use a probe thermometer. The USDA's safe minimums are 165 F for poultry, 160 F for ground meat, and 145 F for whole cuts of pork and fish. A wood oven does not heat evenly, so check the thickest part.
  • Plan the day backwards. Pizza when the fire is out, bread as it falls, then a pot of beans in the cooling oven overnight.
Safety: burn only dry untreated hardwood, wear long oven mitts, keep children and pets back, and never leave a lit oven alone.
