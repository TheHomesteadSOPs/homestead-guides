Seed Starting Calculator

Enter your average last spring frost date and get your own calendar: when to start each crop indoors, when to set it out, and what to sow straight into the garden. Timing comes from the University of New Hampshire Extension planting chart.

How the dates are worked out

UNH Extension's rule is to count back from the day a seedling goes outside, not from the frost date itself: "count backwards the number of weeks the seed takes to grow from sowing. This will be the date you will start your seeds." Cool-season crops like cabbage and onions go out before the last frost, and warm-season crops like tomatoes and peppers go out after it, so the calculator first finds each crop's set-out window and then subtracts its weeks indoors.

A worked example. With a last frost of May 15, tomatoes go out 1-2 weeks after it (May 22 to May 29). Count back 6-8 weeks and you sow them indoors between March 27 and April 17. Peppers go out 2 weeks after the frost (May 29), so at 8-10 weeks indoors they start March 20 to April 3.

The frost date is an average, so in some years the last frost comes later. UNH's own note for central New Hampshire is that Memorial Day is a conservative estimate. Do not set out frost-tender plants before the date, and give every transplant a week or two of hardening off first.

Common questions

When should I start tomato seeds indoors?

UNH Extension gives tomatoes 6-8 weeks indoors and sets them out 1-2 weeks after the last frost, so start them about 4-7 weeks before your last frost date. With a May 15 last frost, that is roughly March 27 to April 17.

Which seeds do I start first?

Onions, leeks and celery come first (8-12 weeks indoors), then parsley, peppers and eggplant. Tomatoes and basil follow, then the cabbage family and lettuce, and cucumbers, squash and melons go last at only 3-4 weeks.

Do I count back from the frost date or the planting date?

From the day the seedlings go outside. UNH says to count backwards the number of weeks the seed takes to grow from sowing. Cool crops like cabbage go out before the last frost and warm crops like peppers go out after it, so their start dates shift accordingly. This calculator does that for you.

Which vegetables should not be started indoors?

Peas, radishes, turnips, beets, carrots, sweet corn, beans and parsnips are sown straight into the garden. Potatoes go in as seed potatoes and sweet potatoes as rooted slips.
